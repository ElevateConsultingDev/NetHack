# Conception: why this memory exists

The unconformity marker. Everything before this happened off-record, in one Claude Code session on 2026-09-23 and 2026-09-24 and in the git log of branch `ai-player`.

## The problem
A NetHack 3.6.7 fork with a socket channel (`src/aipipe.c`) is played by a Python autopilot: a deterministic ENGINE (checks, mechanics, routines, standing orders) and a BRAIN (a model, Claude Haiku, later also a local Qwen; a rule brain as fallback) that sets standing orders and answers escalations and checkpoints. The goal is to ascend. On 2026-09-23 the pilot averaged about Dlvl 3 to 3.6 and 81% of games ended in a stall.

## What the brain's memory was before this
A single file, `pilot/journal.md`, rewritten wholesale after each batch by `pilot.reflect` from the batch's records. Its lessons could not be traced to the runs that produced them, and each rewrite destroyed the previous understanding. Two A/B tests (Haiku with and without the journal, 32 games each on seed 3000) measured its effect on depth at +0.31 and then -0.41 levels, both within a margin of 0.35: about zero.

## The initial strategy
The journal as it stood on 2026-09-24 becomes the first `conclusions.md` stratum, unchanged in substance. Its brain lessons: never fight the Watch or shopkeepers; Elbereth is a gamble, not a harbor; step_away does not outrun fast monsters; never rest with a hostile in view; at critical HP act, do not open inventory; never melee a floating eye; searching burns food; descend about one level per XL.

## What happened in the engine meanwhile (off-record, in git)
28 loop iterations, 11 kept: prayer gate; unattended fallback to rules; non-dwarves leave the Mines; fetching thrown daggers past floating eyes; food list and tins; shop stock is never loot (the largest gain, +0.98 levels on held-out seeds); armor; corpses judged by the monster table; fighting out when engulfed; remembered dark floor; stale stairs forgotten; probe loops fixed. Average deepest on the fixed test seeds went from about 4.1 to about 5.6.

## Why a Stratigraph
Dave's design (STRATIGRAPH.md, adapted from Jeremy Iglehart's genome): events are immutable, conclusions are versioned and archived before every change, the live edge is regenerable. It should give the brain traceable lessons and let the record show how the understanding moved.
